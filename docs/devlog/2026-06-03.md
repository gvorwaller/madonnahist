### Key Technical Insight
The correction lexicon's word-diff must compare the LLM draft (Claude cleanup) against the corrected text, not the raw OCR (Google Vision). The raw OCR text from full-page bounding-box mapping has words in spatial order (left-to-right, top-to-bottom across the whole page, jumbled across cells), while the corrected text is in reading order within one cell. Edit-distance alignment between these two produces only insertions/deletions — the `if (!ocrTok || !corrTok) continue` filter skips them all, resulting in zero lexicon entries.

Even after switching to the Claude draft (which is in reading order), a character-level similarity filter is essential. Without it, the word-level alignment maps positionally-coincident but semantically-unrelated tokens as substitution pairs ("Je"→"pie") when the word counts differ. The 40% edit-distance threshold filters these out while keeping real OCR garbles ("Nirdac"→"Nordic", distance 2 on length 6 = 33%).
