### Key Technical Insight

1. **LLM hallucination on product references is real and costly.** I fabricated a Daige Pro-Easel with a made-up daige.com URL; cited the wrong G9 II resolution; recommended cotton gloves backwards (an entrenched myth that NARA and LoC explicitly contradict); cited B&H SKUs that turned out to be discontinued/replacement slots looking live to a casual fetch. The user caught the Daige fabrication directly; the rest required Codex's adversarial review to surface. Pattern-matching on plausible-sounding archival language is exactly the failure mode that produces these. **Spec-first product descriptions** ("100W bi-color COB Bowens-mount monolight, CRI 95+/TLCI 95+") are more durable than specific SKUs that rotate every 12 months.

2. **B&H product URLs can be silent replacement pages.** A URL like `1753603-REG/amaran_apm021xa10_cob_100x_s_bi_color.html` looks like a live product page but, when actually visited at checkout time, redirects to a "this product is replaced by..." landing. Casual verification (does the URL return 200? does the page mention the product name?) doesn't catch this. The only catch is either WebFetching the live page and parsing the replacement banner, or asking someone (Codex) to manually inspect.

3. **The codex-rescue agent (subagent_type) is the right tool, not the relay.** I initially used the relay MCP to message a peer Codex session. User correctly pointed out the codex-rescue agent does this directly with a clean prompt-and-report-back loop, no relay timing/peer-name ambiguity. Lesson: when the goal is "have Codex review X and report back," the agent is the right tool; the relay is for ongoing peer-to-peer messaging across separate sessions.

4. **Cotton gloves are a paper-handling myth.** Both NARA and LoC explicitly say clean dry hands for paper records — gloves reduce dexterity and cause more tear/snag damage than the trace skin oils they prevent. Gloves are for photographs, metal, or contaminated material. This was wrong in three different places in the doc (Misc/Preservation table, Order 3 Gaylord, Sources) and propagated because it sounded right.

5. **A doc claiming "conservation-lab standard arrangement" should actually meet that bar.** The original equipment-shortlist had archival-sounding language ("forensic-grade archival masters," "conservation-lab standard arrangement," "non-negotiable") that wasn't actually defended by the cited authorities. Codex's #1 nitpick was this exact pattern. The fix was an explicit Scope and Honesty Disclaimer section: what's borrowed from FADGI/NEDCC/LoC, what's home-practical, what we're explicitly *not* trying to hit. That separation is what lets a future reader (or the user, in 2 years) decide what to trust.
